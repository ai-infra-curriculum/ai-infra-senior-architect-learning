# MOD-402: Executive Leadership and Communication

**Level**: Senior Architect (Strategic / Executive)
**Duration**: 35-50 hours
**Prerequisites**: Architect-level experience; mod-401 (Enterprise AI Strategy)

## Module Overview

This module is part of the Senior AI Infrastructure Architect curriculum
(level 400). Focus: executive leadership.

Senior-architect modules emphasize executive communication, strategic
thinking, and organizational influence — not hands-on implementation.
For hands-on technical material on related topics, see the engineer-level
and senior-engineer-level repos.

### Executive Summary

MOD-401 taught you to *build* a strategy. MOD-402 teaches you to *carry
it* — through boards, C-suites, skeptical CFOs, line-of-business VPs,
and a technical organization that has to execute the thing. The failure
mode at this level is almost never "the strategy was wrong on paper." It
is "the architect could not get the room, could not hold the room, or
lost the room when the first objection landed." This module is the
communication, influence, and organizational-change half of the senior
architect's job.

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

- Build and project executive presence in board, C-suite, and
  industry-forum settings
- Construct narratives that survive translation across technical,
  financial, and risk-focused audiences
- Run a stakeholder-engagement plan that converts blockers into
  sponsors over a 90-day horizon
- Lead organizational change at scale using an established change model
  (Kotter, ADKAR, or a defensible adaptation)
- Make and defend high-stakes decisions under uncertainty, including
  crisis scenarios where the stakes are reputational or regulatory
- Build and develop a high-performing architecture team

## Learning Objectives

### Executive Presence and Credibility

1. **Presence and gravitas**
   - Project confidence without false certainty
   - Handle hostile questions without defensiveness
   - Match register to audience (board vs. all-hands vs. 1:1)
   - Recover from mistakes publicly and credibly

2. **Credibility over time**
   - Build a track record of calibrated predictions
   - Say "I don't know" without losing authority
   - Distinguish strong claims from weak ones in your own speech

### Strategic Storytelling and Narrative

3. **Narrative construction**
   - Apply the Minto Pyramid Principle (answer-first structure)
   - Build a "why now" that holds up to CFO scrutiny
   - Use strategic storytelling frameworks (situation → complication →
     resolution; "what, so what, now what")
   - Design narratives that compress to one sentence and expand to
     fifty pages without losing coherence

4. **Format fluency**
   - 1-page executive brief
   - 3-page detailed brief
   - Board deck (10-25 slides, narrative-driven)
   - Amazon-style 6-page narrative memo + PR/FAQ
   - 90-second verbal pitch (elevator / hallway / Q&A opener)

### C-Level Stakeholder Management

5. **Mapping and influence**
   - Identify sponsors, supporters, neutrals, and blockers
   - Distinguish stated objections from underlying concerns
   - Sequence conversations: who hears what, in what order, before the
     formal decision moment
   - Use pre-wiring — the formal meeting is where decisions are
     *ratified*, not made

6. **Stakeholder-specific framing**
   - CEO: business outcomes, competitive position, narrative legacy
   - CFO: unit economics, TCO, risk-adjusted return, optionality
   - COO: operational reliability, incident impact, team capacity
   - CISO / CRO: attack surface, regulatory exposure, blast radius
   - General Counsel: liability, contracts, IP, regulatory trajectory
   - Board: fiduciary duty, enterprise risk, strategic coherence

### Leading Organizational Change at Scale

7. **Change frameworks**
   - Apply Kotter's 8-step change model to an AI infrastructure
     transformation
   - Use ADKAR (Prosci) for individual-level change readiness
   - Diagnose resistance as information, not opposition

8. **Change at scale**
   - Design communication cascades (board → execs → directors → ICs)
   - Build coalition structures that survive leadership turnover
   - Sequence wins to build momentum (quick, visible, credible)
   - Manage the "valley of despair" between old-state and new-state
     productivity

### Crisis Management and Executive Decision-Making

9. **High-stakes decisions**
   - Separate reversible from irreversible decisions (Bezos one-way vs.
     two-way doors)
   - Pre-mortem analysis for critical bets
   - Decide under incomplete information without freezing

10. **Crisis communication**
    - Hold a position under hostile questioning
    - Communicate during active incidents (internal and external)
    - Manage the gap between "what we know" and "what we're being
      asked to confirm"
    - Rebuild trust after a visible failure

### Building High-Performing Architecture Teams

11. **Team design**
    - Shape architecture roles for scale (principal / staff / senior)
    - Build a career ladder that rewards influence, not just output
    - Establish architecture review practices without becoming a
      bottleneck

12. **Developing successors**
    - Coach architects toward their next level, not yours
    - Delegate decisions, not just execution
    - Create a bench deep enough that you are replaceable

### Week 1: Executive Presence and Credibility (6 hours)

**Topics:**
- The three registers (board / C-suite / organization) and when to use each
- Gravitas as a learned skill, not a trait
- Handling hostile questions: acknowledge → reframe → answer → bridge
- The cost of overclaiming vs. the cost of hedging
- Public recovery from mistakes

**Readings:**
- *The Pyramid Principle* — Barbara Minto (Part I: Logic in Writing)
- HBR: "Executive Presence" (Sylvia Ann Hewlett)
- Nancy Duarte: *Resonate* (selected chapters on audience empathy)

**Exercise:** Record yourself answering three hostile questions on a real
or constructed strategic choice. Review for filler words, hedge language,
and recovery behavior. See `exercises/exercise-01-case-study.md` for a
structured version.

### Week 2: Strategic Storytelling (8 hours)

**Topics:**
- The Minto Pyramid Principle in practice
- Situation / Complication / Resolution (SCR) narrative structure
- Amazon's six-page narrative memo and PR/FAQ format
- Deck design for narrative, not reference
- Compressing: one sentence, one paragraph, one page, one deck

**Readings:**
- *The Pyramid Principle* — Barbara Minto
- *Working Backwards* — Colin Bryar & Bill Carr (narrative memos, PR/FAQ)
- Nancy Duarte: *Slide:ology* or *Resonate*
- *Made to Stick* — Chip & Dan Heath (SUCCESs framework)

**Exercise:** Write a 1-page strategy memo for a real investment ask.
See `exercises/exercise-02-strategy-memo.md`.

**Deliverable:** Three artifacts on the *same* strategic recommendation:
- 1-page executive brief
- 3-page detailed brief
- Amazon-style 6-page narrative memo

They must be internally consistent and should get sharper, not longer,
as the format expands.

**Topics:**

**Readings:**

## Case Studies

### Case Study 1: Microsoft's AI Transformation under Satya Nadella

**Background:**
- CEO transition in 2014 after a decade of stagnation
- Mobile-and-cloud-first repositioning, then AI-first
- Cultural reset around "growth mindset" and empathy
- $10B+ OpenAI partnership and subsequent product integration <!-- needs-research: confirm cumulative publicly disclosed investment figure and dates -->

**Your Role:** Imagine you are the senior AI infrastructure architect
inside Microsoft in 2016, briefing Satya on a multi-year platform bet.

**Assignment:**
1. Analyze the leadership communication patterns that enabled the
   transformation (public writing, earnings calls, internal memos) using
   only primary sources
2. Identify three practices you would port into your own organization
3. Identify one practice that would *not* travel — and why

**Deliverables:**
- Communication-pattern analysis (10 pages)
- Three-practice adoption plan for your organization
- One-practice non-adoption memo with reasoning

**Success Criteria:**
- Primary sources only (Nadella's own writing / talks / interviews,
  Microsoft earnings transcripts, SEC filings)
- Distinction between what Microsoft did and what *worked*
- Honest assessment of transferability

### Case Study 2: Netflix — Architecture Evolution and Communication

**Background:**
- Public evolution from DVD-by-mail to streaming to global content
- Deliberately public engineering culture (tech blog, conference talks)
- "Context, not control" and the Netflix culture memo
- Architecture decisions communicated as narrative, not org charts

**Your Role:** Lead architect responsible for externalizing your own
organization's technical story — to candidates, partners, regulators,
and the market.

**Assignment:**
1. Analyze Netflix's public architecture communication over time
2. Build a 12-month external-communication plan for your organization
3. Identify the risks of communicating too much (competitive, regulatory,
   reputational)

**Deliverables:**
- Communication strategy document (10-12 pages)
- 12-month editorial calendar (blog, talks, papers)
- Risk register for public communication

**Success Criteria:**
- Clear theory of audience (who are you writing for, and why)
- Legal and competitive review integrated, not bolted on
- Measurable goals (hiring, partnership inbound, regulatory posture)

---

### Case Study 3: Amazon — "Working Backwards" Decision Framework

**Background:**
- PR/FAQ as a mechanism for forcing clarity before code
- Six-page narrative memos replacing slide decks in most decision forums
- One-way-door vs. two-way-door decision taxonomy
- Leadership Principles as an operating system, not posters

**Your Role:** You have been asked to introduce *one* Amazon-style
practice into a traditional enterprise that currently runs on 60-slide
decks and status meetings.

**Assignment:**
1. Choose one practice (PR/FAQ, 6-pager, one-way doors, pre-mortem) and
   justify the choice for this organization specifically
2. Design a 90-day rollout
3. Anticipate failure modes (cargo-culting, political backlash,
   leadership drift)

**Deliverables:**
- Choice-and-rationale memo (3 pages)
- 90-day rollout plan
- Failure-mode register with mitigation

**Success Criteria:**
- Honest about what will *not* transfer from Amazon's context
- Rollout survives a change of sponsor
- Measurement plan separates adoption from impact

## Assessment

### Module Quiz

Short strategic-thinking questions — no crisp right answers, defend your
position. See `quiz.md` for the full prompt list.

### Strategic Leadership Portfolio (8-10 hours)

A single coherent artifact set built around *one* strategic
recommendation you would make in a real organization:

1. **1-page executive brief** (for the CEO)
2. **3-page detailed brief** (for the CFO + CRO)
3. **Amazon-style 6-page narrative memo**
4. **Board deck** (10-15 slides)
5. **Stakeholder map + 90-day engagement plan**
6. **Organizational change roadmap excerpt** (the first 12 months)

All six artifacts must be about the same recommendation and must be
internally consistent. A reviewer should be able to spot-check any two
and find the same core argument.

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- **Narrative coherence** (25%) — same story at every altitude
- **Audience fit** (20%) — each artifact targets the right reader
- **Executive communication** (20%) — answer-first, no burying the lede
- **Stakeholder strategy** (15%) — pre-wiring plan is credible
- **Change realism** (10%) — the roadmap survives first contact
- **Completeness** (10%) — all six artifacts present and polished
